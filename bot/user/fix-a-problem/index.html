Fix a problem

Each heading is a condition. Find yours, try the fix, then rerun the command from the head build page.

If the check says a device is missing

Read the reason it prints. “No such file /dev/spidev0.0” means SPI is not enabled: rerun sudo raspi-config and turn it on, then reboot. “No I2C device at 0x40” means the pan-tilt board isn’t answering: check SDA and SCL aren’t swapped, and that the board’s VCC is on 3.3 V.

If the eyes stay black

Both power off, then check the eye’s VCC is on a 3.3 V pin, not 5 V (a 5 V pin can kill the display, and it will look like this). Check BLK is on 3.3 V too: no backlight looks exactly like no picture. Then check CS: the left eye’s CS goes to GPIO 8 and the right eye’s to GPIO 7, and each display needs its own. Two displays on one CS show the same thing or nothing.

If the eyes are lit but look wrong: a dark pill on a bright background, or the picture shifted off one edge

The display’s colour inversion or pixel offset differs from the module we wrote the driver for. Both are one-line driver settings (invert, x_offset, y_offset in robot/hal/drivers/st7789.py). Open an issue with a photo and the listing you bought, and it becomes a flag.

If only one eye works

Swap the two displays’ CS wires. If the problem moves with the wire, the wiring is wrong on that side. If it stays with the display, that display is bad. Sold as 2-packs with no spare, so you may be buying another pack.

If the eyes are sideways, mirrored, or swapped

That’s expected the first time. Use --rotate, --mirror, and --swap as described on the head page. Nothing is wrong with the hardware.

If the mouth never lights

In order: the bench 5 V supply is on and its negative is tied to a Pi GND pin; the level shifter’s pin 1 is tied to GND (with it floating, the chip outputs nothing); the data wire goes into the panel’s DIN, not DOUT (the pads look alike); the panel’s own 5 V and GND are connected.

If you are trying to drive the mouth from the Pi, stop: it cannot work. rpi_ws281x lights these pixels by taking over the BCM283x PWM and DMA blocks, and a Pi 5 has neither, its GPIO being on the separate RP1. You get ws2811_init failed with code -3, on every pin, and no wiring change helps. Changing pins was once suggested here; it was wrong, and there is no pin that works.

The mouth is driven by the face controller, which lights these panels with PIO. The bench tool now says so in one line instead of crashing.

If the mouth lights but the pattern is scrambled

The panel layout guess is wrong, not the wiring. Watch the layout pattern and follow the rule on the head page: snaking rows mean --serpentine, a backwards second panel means --rotate-second.

If the mouth flickers or shows wrong colours

The data signal is marginal. The usual causes: the level shifter is skipped or wired without its GND, the data wire is long and unpaired (run it alongside a ground wire), or the 5 V supply is sagging (a phone charger with a cut cable is not a 3 A supply).

If the camera isn’t detected

If your camera is the Arducam version of Camera Module 3 (the parts list allows it), it needs two config lines the official camera doesn’t. In your ssh session:

If it’s plugged into the CAM/DISP 0 connector, use dtoverlay=imx708,cam0 instead. This is Arducam’s own documented requirement; their 12MP IMX708 guide has the long version.

If it’s still not found, the Pi is probing and the camera isn’t answering, which means the cable. Power off fully, open both connector latches, and reseat: at the Pi end the ribbon’s metal contacts face the middle of the board, away from the Ethernet and USB jacks; at the camera end they face the camera’s circuit board. Straight in, latch closed, and a gentle tug must not move it. A cable seated at a slight angle reads as no camera, and so does a flipped end. Use a Pi 5 camera cable (22-pin at the Pi end), not a Pi 4 one. Then rpicam-still --list-cameras should list the imx708. The kernel’s view of the conversation is dmesg | grep -i imx708: “failed to read chip id” means wiring, silence means the config lines above are missing.

If the AI HAT shows on lspci but there’s no /dev/hailo0

The wrong driver family is installed. hailo-all supports the older Hailo-8 boards; the AI HAT+ 2 is a Hailo-10H and needs hailo-h10-all:

Afterwards ls /dev/hailo0 exists and the check says ai-hat: ok.

If the servos buzz, jitter, or reboot the Pi

The servos are on the Pi’s 5 V. Move V+ to the bench supply. Jitter at rest with a proper supply usually means the arm is mounted so the centre position is near an end stop: pull the arm off, centre the servo with the head command, refit the arm straight.

If the shutter switch reads backwards

COM and NO are swapped, or the switch is a normally-closed type. Move the wire to the other outer terminal.

If uv sync fails on the Pi

The hardware add-ons compile two small libraries. Make sure the apt line from Prepare the Pi ran, especially build-essential, python3-dev, swig, and liblgpio-dev, then run uv sync --extra hardware again.

Still need help? Open an issue at github.com/getmaipai/bot/issues with the exact command you ran and what it printed.
