Hardware record

This page is the budget. A new device names its pin, address, or port here before it is bought. Numbers are marked M (measured on the real part, by a person), D (derived from listings or arithmetic; verify before cutting or ordering), or S (stated by a listing, unconfirmed). A confident wrong number is worse than an admitted gap.

When the Pico 2 face controller is fitted (see below) it takes over the eyes and the mouth, freeing GPIO 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 19 and 25. Both allocations stay in this table: the Pi-driven path still works and is what the bench uses today.

The mic-cut switch left the design with the camera shutter on 2026-09-01: the mic and camera are muted in software now, by voice or the heart knob.

I2C address map

0x15 CST816S (heart knob fallback only), 0x20 MCP23017, 0x2D UPS HAT (E) MCU (M: confirmed on the bench 2026-08-30), 0x29 all VL53L5CX ToF boards (hence the mux), 0x36 both AS5600 head joint encoders (fixed address, no strap pins, hence the mux as well), 0x40 PCA9685, 0x44 INA228 (strapped), 0x5A MPR121, 0x68 MPU-6050 IMU (AD0 low; 0x69 if a second is ever fitted, and the Pi 5’s RTC is on its own connector so it does not contend), 0x71 TCA9548A (strapped up from its 0x70 default; see the conflict below). Keep the bus at 100 to 400 kHz with short stubs; the mux can segment more than the ToFs if it gets flaky.

Mux channel budget (D, 2026-09-01). Eight channels. The seven VL53L5CX ToFs each need one of their own because they all answer at 0x29. The two AS5600 encoders are fixed at 0x36, so they must not share a channel with each other — but each may share a channel with a ToF, since 0x29 and 0x36 do not collide. That is seven channels used and one spare, not nine.

Conflict found 2026-08-31, not previously recorded: the PCA9685 also answers on its All Call address, 0x70 by default and enabled out of reset, which is the TCA9548A multiplexer’s address. The symptom would be ToF reads corrupted whenever a servo is addressed. Fix at both ends, and do BOTH: strap the mux to 0x71 (its A0/A1/A2 pins select 0x70 to 0x77, so the collision can be designed out rather than programmed around, and a hardware fix does not depend on software running correctly), and clear the ALLCALL bit in MODE1 during driver init, and strap the multiplexer’s A0 high to move it to 0x71. Verify on the bench by connecting only the PCA9685 and running i2cdetect -y 1; seeing both 0x40 and 0x70 from one board confirms it.

Face controller (Raspberry Pi Pico 2, RP2350)

Its own pin budget, because it is a second board and not the Pi’s header. It connects to the Pi by USB on the 4-port hub, is flashed once at build time, and speaks the line protocol in firmware/face/. Design record: Face controller.

Panel VCC and BLK on 3V3(OUT) (pin 36); grounds on pins 18 and 23, one inside each signal group. The mouth matrix takes its 5 V from the peripheral buck, never from the Pico. System clock 125 MHz, eye SPI 62.5 MHz (the ST7789’s own 16 ns write-cycle ceiling); 125 MHz is what makes 62.5 land exactly on the RP2350’s SPI divider. D, none of it measured yet. The firmware compiles for the target (63 kB flash, 38 kB RAM of the RP2350’s 520 kB); everything about how it behaves on real panels is still unverified.

Power rails

36 V pack (10S, ~360 Wh, 21700 cells, BMS 20 A+, 30 A breaker inside the bay) → 36-to-12 V reducer (15 A / 180 W, sized for a ~126 W worst case plus the brake-release coils) → fused 12 V distribution block → hardwired 12 V-to-USB-C PD converter into the UPS HAT (the Pi’s supply), a 5 V peripheral buck (8 to 10 A: mouth, sensors, fan, speaker power), a separate 5 V servo buck (2 to 3 A, isolated so servo inrush cannot brown out logic), and the 12 V WS2815 underglow directly. LED brightness caps are load-bearing: the mouth’s 128 px at full white is 7.7 A.

XVF3800 mic array firmwareShips broken, settled 2026-08-31. A board received on 2.0.6 captured unintelligible audio (“robotic and underwater”) while playback, the LED direction display and the audio levels all looked correct, so nothing pointed at firmware. Measured: zero-crossing rate 0.40 on loud passages against 0.088 once fixed, and stock openWakeWord hey_jarvis scoring 0.0007 against 0.998. Channel 0/1 correlation stayed at +0.885, which is what proves it is device-side: any byte or framing error would drive that to zero. Seeed fixed it in v2.0.10 (USB recovery after bus resets and speed changes, software-PLL state). Unplugging and replugging recovers it until the next boot; a software reset does not, because VBUS never drops. Flashing v2.1.0 fixes it for good: verified 2026-08-31 by rebooting with the array attached, zcr 0.092 and hey_jarvis 0.9986 with no replug. The mixer setting survived the flash and the rebootM
XVF3800 direction of arrivalThe robot reads the auto-selected beam azimuth (xvf_host AEC_AZIMUTH_VALUES, fourth value) at wake and speech onset and stamps it on voice events (hal/drivers/doa.py). Three things are open, all verify on hardware: the azimuth frame’s zero direction and sense against robot-forward (offset_deg/mirrored ship as the identity), whether the control interface answers for the service user without sudo (the build page installs the tool for root; a udev rule may be needed), and the mount. Recommended mount is body-fixed, below the pan joint: DoA then needs no head-yaw compensation and the USB lead never crosses the rotating neck. A head mount instead requires adding the pan angle at capture time before the bearing reaches attentionD
WS2812B mouth from the Pi 5Does not work, settled 2026-08-31. rpi_ws281x takes over the BCM283x PWM and DMA blocks; the Pi 5 has neither, its GPIO being on the RP1. ws2811_init returns -3 (“hardware revision is not supported”) on any pin, then the library segfaults tearing down a struct it never initialised. Not a pin or wiring problem, and GPIO20/SPI1 is not a fallback because the library never gets that far. The driver now refuses on a Pi 5 before calling in. The mouth is driven by the face controller, where PIO does it nativelyM
MPU-6050 IMU (bought)6-DOF: three axes of acceleration and three of rotation, I2C 0x68. Enough for tip detection, being picked up, and knowing the robot was moved while asleep. It has no magnetometer, so there is no absolute heading: integrated gyro yaw drifts, and heading has to come from the lidar or wheel odometry rather than from this. Do not build anything on this reporting which way the robot facesD
VL53L5CX ToF (2x bought, 7 in the design)Multi-zone, 8x8 per sensor with a wide field, not a single point. Seven of them is 448 zones, which is a coarse depth image rather than seven dots, and it is most of what stereo vision would have given for obstacle work. All share address 0x29, which is why the TCA9548A mux existsD
RPLIDAR C1 (bought)360 degrees, 12 m, USB. Goes on USB2 #2 direct, never behind a hub, for the same latency reason as the mic array. The moving-head pivot put it in a waist slot rather than on the head: a spinning scanner on a panning neck sweeps its own frame of reference. No mounting exists yet, so it is bought and parked until the body is builtD
24 GHz mmWave presence radar (2x)FMCW human micro-motion sensor, UART and GPIO output. Detects a person who is sitting still or asleep by their breathing, which a PIR cannot: a PIR loses a motionless person and reports the room empty. Works in complete darkness, which is exactly when the camera cannot. Bought 2026-09-01. Open, all verify on hardware: UART or the GPIO present/absent line (GPIO costs one pin and gives no range; UART gives distance and motion detail but the Pi’s UARTs are contended); where the two mount (body-fixed front and rear is the assumption, NOT the head, since a presence sensor that sweeps with the neck has the underglow’s problem); and its real range and through-material reach in this houseD
XVF3800 LED ring12 LEDs, firmware-driven, host-controllable over the USB control interface: LED_EFFECT (0 off, 1 breath, 2 rainbow, 3 single colour, 4 doa), LED_COLOR, LED_DOA_COLOR, LED_BRIGHTNESS, LED_SPEED. No per-pixel control, so no chase, spin or custom animation; the vocabulary is three usable effects and a colour. Ships in mode 4, which lights on any sound and is turned off. Direction cannot be driven from the host (the firmware owns it) and is not used on the ring anyway, see design-decisions. Powered from the same 5 V the microphones are, so the mute switch darkens it as a wiring factM
Body controllerA second Pico 2 drives the WS2815 underglow, because a Pi 5 cannot and because the head pans 270 degrees: a one-wire timing-critical signal must not cross a rotating joint. Connects to the Pi by USB serial through the USB3 hub, same as the face controller. Ordered 2026-08-31. Open: whether it shares the face firmware behind a build flag or gets its ownD
AS5600 head joint encoders (2x bought 2026-08-31)12-bit magnetic rotary encoder read over I2C, reporting the angle the neck actually reached rather than the angle the servo was told to hold. A hobby servo is open loop, so from the Pi a stall, a slipped horn, a stripped gear and a clean move are indistinguishable. Fixed at 0x36 with no strap pins (the addressable variant is the AS5600L; these are not), so the two cannot share a mux channel with each other — see the channel budget above. Also supplies the pan angle a head-mounted mic array needs at capture time, which the IMU cannot give (no magnetometer, so gyro yaw drifts). Open, all verify on hardware: the magnet mount — the part wants a diametrically magnetised magnet centred on the rotating shaft at a small axial gap, and the Yahboom bracket may not expose a shaft end behind the servo, in which case the magnet bonds to the rotating bracket and the board is held fixed and concentric above it; which joints get one, since two were bought and pan is the joint DoA needs; and the zero reference against robot-forwardD

AI HAT+ 2 bundled header: top is a flat socket, no pins through (as the design assumed)SOLVED 2026-08-31 by REPLACING the 16 mm stacking header that ships with the AI HAT+ 2 with an extra-tall 2x20 one. The stock 16 mm header reaches the HAT and no further, so its pins fill the visible top wells. The Pi’s own 40-pin header is soldered and is never touched. The tall header’s socket takes the Pi’s pins and its long legs pass through the HAT and stand proud on top for the ribbon. The HAT rides higher, so the standoffs build up from the M2.5 kit. Verified by ai-hat: ok (/dev/hailo0, HAILO10H) with the header fitted. Pressing pins into the HAT’s socket from above does NOT work: the Pi’s own pins already occupy the wells, and a Dupont jumper is far too short to reach a contactM

Measurements the base stage needs before anything is cut

Between-fenders span and stripped axle-plate span of the donor hoverboard (the deck is sized from them); wheel diameter (the 6.3 in listing figure vs 165 mm from the firmware community, 3 percent on every brake number); the tallest threshold in the house; the battery bay and side wall with calipers; phase resistance and Kv of the motors. None of these are measured yet.
